No. Once a case is ongoing and has had case conferences and motions, the court will not dismiss it. It is not uncommon for a family law case to take years to complete — that is normal.

A client came to consult me. Her case started several years ago and still is not finished. Her previous lawyer told her that because it had dragged on so long, the court might close the case. She was worried, and came to ask whether that was true.

The short answer: no. Once a case is underway, the court will not close it on its own.

---

## 1. Limitation Periods Govern Claims That Have Not Yet Been Started

An [equalization](/en/blog/equalization-limitation-chinese-lawyer-ontario/) claim must be started within the earliest of three deadlines: six years after separation, two years after a divorce, or six months after a spouse passes away — after that, the court will not hear it. Her case was started years ago. That door is already passed, and the limitation period has nothing more to do with her.

---

## 2. Once a Case Is Open, the Court Does Not Close It on Its Own

A case in the court system stays open. Taking a long time does not get it closed — the court does not issue a dismissal order just because years have passed. Once a case is ongoing and has had case conferences and motions, the court will not dismiss it. It is not uncommon for a family law case to take years to complete, and that is normal. If your case has been ongoing for several years, do not worry — follow the court's instructions and take the steps that move the case forward. If anything, an older case gets priority when trial dates are set, because it has been waiting longer than the others.

---

## 3. Only the Parties Together Can End the Case

To close a case, both sides must apply to the court to withdraw it. One side alone cannot end it, and the court will not close it for them. The other way a case ends is by settlement: once the parties have settled, they can file a consent motion asking the court for a consent order, and move forward on an uncontested basis to have the case closed finally.

---

## 4. This Is Not the Same as a Simple Divorce Application

If a [simple application](/en/blog/simple-divorce-filed-dismissed-ontario/) is filed with the court and the applicant does not move the case forward but abandons it, the court will issue a notice of dismissal after two years, giving the party notice to take further steps. If the applicant does not file the required documents within the notice period, the court will finally issue a dismissal. In that case, the case is closed. If either party wants to apply for divorce, a new case must be filed instead.

---

## FAQs

### 1. The other side keeps delaying and refusing to cooperate — will the case just drag on forever?

It will not be closed, but it will not move forward on its own either. If negotiation goes nowhere, move the case through the court process: bring a motion, attend conferences, get a trial date. That is what the process is for.

### 2. Will changing lawyers affect my case?

No. [Changing lawyers](/en/blog/change-lawyer-recalculate-nfp-ontario/) only changes who represents you. The case stays the same, the court file number stays the same, and the process continues. The file just needs to be transferred.

### 3. My case has been going for years — will that hurt me at trial?

No. The court understands that family law cases take time, especially when property is disputed. An older case may even get priority for a trial date. What matters is that you follow the court's directions and keep moving the case forward.
