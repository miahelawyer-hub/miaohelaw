No. Changing title to a jointly owned house requires both owners' signatures. Without your signature, his half can't be sold or transferred either. And a private agreement he signs behind your back doesn't change the title registration.

A client came to consult me. She and her spouse owned [houses in both names](/en/blog/equalization-limitation-joint-title-sold-house-ontario/), half each. Someone on his side had been cooperating with him in [moving assets around](/en/blog/dissipation-of-assets/), and her greatest worry was this: could he quietly sell his half to that person behind her back?

The short answer: no. Without your signature, title can't change.

---

## 1. Title in Two Names Can't Be Changed by One Person Alone

The house is registered in both names, and any dealing with it — sale, transfer, conveyance — requires both owners to sign. His "half" is not a separate piece that can be cut off and sold on its own: there is one title registration, your name is on it, and without your signature the land registry cannot transfer the property — and no legitimate lawyer will process it for him. The very first door he would have to pass is your signature.

---

## 2. A Private Agreement to "Sell" His Half Can't Change Title

He can sign a [private agreement](/en/blog/said-house-money-yours-wont-sign-agreement-ontario/) behind your back, purporting to sell his half to someone. It changes nothing. A private agreement binds only the people who signed it; it cannot alter the land registration — on title, the house still belongs to the two of you. The person who paid real money under that agreement has bought a piece of paper, not half a house. If he wants his money back, his claim is against the person who sold it to him — it doesn't reach your house.

---

## 3. The Extreme Case: What If Someone Forges a Signature and Transfers the House?

In rare cases, someone does take that risk — arranging for a person to impersonate the spouse, sign, and transfer the house away. That transfer is illegal: the court will treat the transfer as invalid, and the title insurance purchased when the home was bought will compensate you for your loss. Even in the worst case, the value of your share can be recovered. You don't need to live in fear of it.

For one more layer of protection, a lawyer can [register a notice](/en/blog/house-in-his-name-designate-matrimonial-home-ontario/) on the house: the property may not be dealt with without your consent. Registration takes effect immediately, anyone searching title will see it, and after that he is even less able to move the property.

---

## FAQs

### 1. He "sold" his half to a friend, and the friend showed up with the agreement, saying he owns a share of the house. What do I do?

Tell him to search the title: the house is still registered in both spouses' names. A private agreement can't change the registration — his friend bought a piece of paper, not half a house. If the friend wants his money back, he claims it from the person who sold it to him. It has nothing to do with you.

### 2. Our title is joint tenancy, not set half-and-half shares. Is it the same?

Yes. Whether you hold the property as joint tenants or as tenants in common with stated shares, changing title requires every owner on title to sign. No single owner can transfer or sell anything alone.

### 3. I'm still uneasy. Is there anything I can do in advance?

Yes. Have a lawyer register a notice on the property stating it may not be dealt with without your consent. Registration takes effect right away, and any lawyer who searches title will see your name and the notice at once.
