It depends on whether it's still the [family residence](/en/blog/matrimonial-home-title-vs-property-division-ontario/) on separation day. If yes, the wedding-day value can't be deducted — the whole home counts as the matrimonial home. Only a pre-separation [sale](/en/blog/premarital-home-sold-ontario-deduction/), move-out, or rental may bring the deduction back. A post-separation [purchase](/en/blog/buy-house-after-separation-still-divide-ontario/) isn't counted twice but must be disclosed; if the equalization can't otherwise be paid, the court can [order it sold](/en/blog/force-sale-matrimonial-home-ontario/).

---

## 1. Still the Family Residence on Separation Day: No Deduction

A home bought before marriage normally qualifies for a wedding-day value deduction — only the post-marriage appreciation gets shared.

But there's an exception: if it's still your family residence on separation day. The matrimonial home doesn't get that deduction. The whole property counts as the matrimonial home, and not a dollar of wedding-day value comes off.

So "bought before marriage" doesn't mean "the pre-marriage portion is mine." What matters is whether you're still living in it on separation day.

---

## 2. Sold, Vacated, or Rented Before Separation: Deduction May Come Back

Sold before separation: it's no longer the family residence on separation day, so the wedding-day value can be deducted.

Moved out or rented out: same — no longer the family residence, deduction available.

But the move has to happen before separation. After separation, it's too late.

---

## 3. A New Home Bought After Separation Isn't Counted Twice — But Must Be Disclosed

Buy after separation with money that was in the account on separation day. Think of [separation day](/en/blog/net-family-property-ontario/) as a photograph of both parties' finances: whatever was in the accounts and whatever was owed that day is what counts. How the money moves or the market [shifts](/en/blog/house-appreciation-after-separation-ontario/) afterward doesn't change the photograph.

That money was already counted once. Counting the new home's value again would be double-counting, so the new home isn't recalculated.

But the purchase must be disclosed — it can't be hidden.

If the final order requires an equalization payment you can't otherwise pay, the court can order the new home sold to satisfy it. If you can pay, the court will normally leave a post-separation purchase alone.

---

## What to Do

If you're still living in a pre-marriage home, don't assume "the pre-marriage portion is mine." If it's the family residence on separation day, there's no deduction.

To preserve the wedding-day deduction, act before separation: sell, or move out and rent it out.

A post-separation purchase must be disclosed. If you can't otherwise pay the equalization owed, the court can order the new home sold.

Fix the [separation date](/en/blog/separation-date-vs-divorce-date-ontario/) early, and put it [in writing](/en/blog/said-house-money-yours-wont-sign-agreement-ontario/).

## FAQs

### 1. I bought the home before marriage and paid the down payment alone. Can I deduct the wedding-day value?

It depends on whether it's the family residence on separation day. If yes, no deduction — the whole home counts as the matrimonial home. Who paid the down payment doesn't change this rule.

### 2. After separation I used account funds to buy a house. Can my spouse still claim it?

The funds were already counted once on separation day — no double-counting. But the purchase must be disclosed. If you owe an equalization payment you can't otherwise pay, the court can order the new home sold. If you can pay, the court will normally leave it alone.

### 3. The house was rented out before separation. Can I get the wedding-day deduction back?

Yes. Once rented out, it's no longer the family residence, so the wedding-day value can be deducted. But the move must happen before separation.
