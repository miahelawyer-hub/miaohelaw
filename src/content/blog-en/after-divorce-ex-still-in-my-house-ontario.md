The house is in one name. That person paid. The other party has lived there for years with a child. The marriage is over. Can I have the house back?

You can negotiate, and you can sue. Do not read “my name on title” as “nothing in the NFP.” How title is registered was never the same as how the house is equalized. See [title versus property division](/en/blog/matrimonial-home-title-vs-property-division-ontario/).

## After the divorce, the ex is still in my house. What now?

Ask first whether you are still spouses.

During the marriage, both spouses have an equal right to possession of a matrimonial home, regardless of title. Section 21 also bars a sale or mortgage without the other spouse's consent or a court order. Changing the locks, or putting the other spouse on the street, is usually both unlawful and damaging to the file.

Section 19(2) is plain: if only one name is on title, the other person can live there **because you are still spouses**, not because they own a share of the house. That right ends when you stop being spouses — unless a separation agreement or a court order says they can stay. Once a foreign divorce is recognized, you are former spouses in Canada. If they still live there, it is usually because you have not yet required them to leave, not because the statute still gives them a spouse's right to occupy.

A recognized divorce does not mean you can show up tomorrow and throw them out. Write first. Agree a move-out date. If they will not go, ask the court for an order. Do not change the locks on your own. If they still claim a share of the house, moving out and dividing the money may be decided together.

## I paid in full. Do I still have to share it?

On the NFP statement, often yes.

Ontario does not slice the house into registered halves. It [equalizes net family property](/en/blog/net-family-property-ontario/). The value of the home during the marriage is usually an asset on the titled spouse's statement, then set off against the other spouse's NFP. Paying cash, discharging the mortgage early, and holding title in one name all go into the evidence. They do not, by themselves, switch the statute off.

You already divorced in China, and the judgment said nothing about this Canadian house. Ontario can still divide the money — if the time limit has not run. See [limitation periods for equalization](/en/blog/equalization-limitation-chinese-lawyer-ontario/). After that deadline in Family Law Act s. 7(3), living in the house for years does not usually turn into half the title. A different claim — that they paid in and were meant to own a share — needs proof of both. Staying there is not enough.

If a **minor** still lives there, housing stability matters on exclusive possession and sale. An [adult child](/en/blog/adult-child-no-custody-ontario/) is not a legal right to occupy the titled owner's home. You can agree that the child — and the parent who looks after them — stays until graduation or other housing is found. That is an arrangement, not custody.

## Can I let them stay and pay monthly?

Yes. Write it.

Housing, property tax, and a monthly amount can all be settled. That is a voluntary arrangement. It is not a support order, and it is not a completed equalization. A large cash payment and adding a name to title are different gifts: cash can be spent; a name on title is an interest that has to sign for every later sale or refinance.

Do not admit an unagreed buyout in the Answer. Map recognition and limitation first, then decide whether anyone stays. While you are still spouses and the other party will not leave or sell, the main path is [sale and occupation rent](/en/blog/force-sale-matrimonial-home-ontario/).

## Once we are no longer spouses, can I list the house myself?

Section 21 bars disposing of a matrimonial home without the other spouse’s consent or a court order. The key word is **spouse**. After a recognized foreign divorce you are former spouses, so that bar usually no longer stops the titled owner from listing. That is not the same sentence as “the house is in my name, so I cannot sell it during the marriage.”

Three things can still block a sale: an equalization claim still in time — the other party may claim against the net proceeds, and the court often holds sale money in trust; a trust or equitable claim, which can keep title from being cleared; and a certificate of pending litigation or an enforcement registration already on title. “We are divorced, so I can list tomorrow” is clean only when recognition is clear, limitation has run, and there is no trust or registered burden.

Occupation rent is a separate ledger. After separation, one party occupying the home to the exclusion of the other can claim occupation rent. It is discretionary, not automatic market rent. The court looks at who paid property tax, insurance and maintenance, whether a child lived there, how long the claim sat, and whether the excluded party ever moved for a sale. A titled owner who paid all carrying costs and tolerated years of occupation often sees the amount cut, sometimes to nothing. Do not expect “you lived there for ten years rent-free” to win the entire house on its own.

Adding the other party’s name to title is a gift of a real-property interest: later sales and refinances need their signature, and the matrimonial-home consent rules can return in another form. Cash can be spent and the claim can come back. A name on title is harder to unwind. Neither step should be admitted in the Answer first.

## Common questions

### After the divorce, the ex is still in my house. What now?

Separate title, whether you are still spouses, and whether equalization is in time. After a recognized foreign divorce, the s. 19(2) right usually ends. Occupation is not a half-interest.

### I paid in full. Do I still have to share it?

Title is not NFP. The value during the marriage usually still equalizes, subject to limitation and to what the foreign judgment actually did.

### Can I change the locks?

Not while you are still spouses. After you cease to be spouses the possessory right ends. Use writing or a court process for vacant possession.

### The children live there. Does that freeze the house?

Housing for a minor is important. An adult child is not a legal right to stay.

### Can I let them stay and pay monthly?

Yes, by agreement. That is not an automatic support order, and it is not a finished division of title.

### Once we are no longer spouses, can I list it myself?

Section 21 usually no longer blocks it. Still check recognition, equalization limitation, trust, and any pending-litigation certificate on title.

### After years of occupation, is there occupation rent?

It can be claimed. It is not automatic rent. Who paid carrying costs, whether a child lived there, and how long the claim sat all cut the number.
