You have separated. The children live with one parent. The other pays little, or nothing. The property file is not ready — the house is in one name, the market is soft, the other party will not talk. The question becomes: does child support have to wait as well? If we have not claimed it, is the past period gone?

Property and child support are two ledgers. Equalization has the s. 7(3) clocks. Child support does not die on the same six-year line. How table amounts and spousal support differ is on [child and spousal support](/en/family-law/support/). This article is only the unsued period.

## We separated but never sued. Can I still claim past child support?

Often yes. Not automatically the full arrears, and not automatically nothing.

Table support under the Federal Child Support Guidelines is the child's right. After separation, the parent with primary care can claim it without a divorce. Ontario's Family Law Act also covers married spouses and qualifying unmarried parents. Having no court order only means the Family Responsibility Office cannot yet garnish wages. It does not mean the obligation was legally absent for those months.

If a Form 8 is issued two or three years later, the court decides two things: how far back to go (retroactive), and what is payable from now (ongoing). Ongoing support is usually there while the child remains a child of the marriage. The past period is what this article answers.

The Supreme Court in [D.B.S. v. S.R.G., 2006 SCC 37](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2006/2006scc37/2006scc37.html) did not write an expiry date. It asked courts to weigh the reason for delay, the payor's conduct, the children's circumstances, and hardship. Those four together decide the start date and whether the lump sum is reduced.

[Michel v. Graydon, 2020 SCC 24](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2020/2020scc24/2020scc24.html) added that once the child is independent, unpaid support from the years they were entitled is not automatically lost. Whether a university student is still a child of the marriage is [a support question, not a custody question](/en/blog/adult-child-no-custody-ontario/).

## From which day does support run?

The obligation exists when there is a child, a payor, and primary care with one parent. In practice, the separation date is often the starting point for the retroactive discussion. Separation under one roof, or one spouse leaving because of a restraining order, can still anchor the date if care has already shifted.

Without a case, the table amount does not appear in an account. Occasional transfers can be kept; they should be recorded and later credited, not treated as a full release. If the payor's reported income is low, the monthly table figure will be small. That is not the same sentence as “keep no record.” False disclosure is [imputed income](/en/blog/imputed-income-child-support-ontario/), which needs real evidence, not a guess that someone is paid in cash.

Section 7 expenses are a separate column: activities, dental, childcare in defined cases — reasonable and necessary, shared in proportion to income. Pay from your own account and keep invoices. Groceries, rent, and meals out are not s. 7.

Do not bundle spousal support with child support. Similar incomes often produce little or no SSAG spousal support. The child claim still exists. See [SSAG and imputed income](/en/blog/ontario-spousal-support-ssag-imputed-income-misconceptions/).

## If years have passed, how does the court look at it?

The [D.B.S.](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2006/2006scc37/2006scc37.html) working figure of about three years back from effective notice is an analytical tool, not a section of the Limitations Act. No notice, and no explanation for sitting still, raises the risk that the past period is cut: ongoing support from the order or the application date, with little or no retroactive award.

These facts pull the award further back:

- written requests for tax returns, table support, or s. 7 that went unanswered
- a payor who changed jobs, took cash, or failed to disclose, so the recipient could not know the real income
- a reasonable reason not to file: safety, the other party could not be found, a criminal or restraining-order file first, no funds for counsel
- a period in which the children clearly went without amounts that should have been there

A payor who now says a three-year lump sum would bankrupt them is speaking to hardship. That can reduce quantum. It rarely wipes the entire claim. The children's circumstances weigh more than the payor's inconvenience.

Once there is an order, unpaid amounts go to FRO: garnishment, tax intercepts, driver's licence. See [when the other party will not pay](/en/blog/spousal-child-support-ontario/). Without an order, FRO has nothing to collect. The past period has to become a figure in an order or an agreement before enforcement attaches.

A property file can move on its own timetable — whether a post-separation increase is shared is [After separation, if the house goes up, do you still split the gain?](/en/blog/house-appreciation-after-separation-ontario/). Child support is not the same “wait and see.” What you delay is the child's right, not a bargaining chip.

## Does a lawyer's letter count as having claimed it?

Often yes.

Effective notice does not require a court stamp. A lawyer's letter or a clear email that tells the other party the separation date, where the children live, and that table and s. 7 support are being claimed, can later go in front of a judge as proof you did not sit on the right. Non-receipt of money does not cancel notice that was given.

Do not admit an uncalculated figure in the letter, and do not write a release of property or spousal support into it. The point is a record: dates, the claim, the children's expenses. Activities paid from your own card, with invoices against the bank record; ordinary living costs are not recovered through this letter.

If the other party starts transferring money, take it, record it, and do not say orally that the past is closed. A release belongs in an agreement or a consent order. A separation agreement that waives child support may still fail. See [leaving child support out of a separation agreement](/en/blog/child-support-waiver-separation-agreement-ontario/).

## Common questions

### We separated but never sued. Can I still claim past child support?

Often yes. Not automatically in full. [D.B.S.](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2006/2006scc37/2006scc37.html) looks at delay, conduct, the children, and hardship.

### From which day?

The separation date is the usual starting discussion. The actual start date follows notice and evidence, not the day you join the filing queue.

### Two or three years — is the past gone?

Not automatically. “Three years from notice” is not a drop-dead date. Concealment or a reasonable delay can go further. Sitting still can cut the past period.

### A lawyer's letter?

Often effective notice. Non-payment does not mean the claim was never made.

### Low income — is there anything to collect?

The table follows income. That is separate from whether the past period still exists. Without an order, FRO cannot collect.

### Section 7 retroactively?

If the expense was reasonable and necessary, shared by income, with invoices. Groceries are not s. 7.

### The child is now independent?

Unpaid amounts from the years of entitlement can still be claimed. [Michel v. Graydon, 2020 SCC 24](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2020/2020scc24/2020scc24.html). Not always in full. Not never.
