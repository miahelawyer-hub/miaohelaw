You have lived in Canada for years. An email arrives that claims to be from a Chinese court, or a courier notice sits uncollected. The other party has sued for divorce in China, asking only to dissolve the marriage, saying nothing about the Canadian house. Is this a scam? Can an Ontario lawyer answer it? If I never pick up the parcel, can I treat it as if it never happened?

Three ledgers. One is how to answer the Chinese case. Two is whether that court can touch Canadian property. Three is the Ontario limitation clock once China grants a divorce.

---

## Sued for divorce in China while you live in Canada. What now?

Treat it as real. Then decide how to answer.

A Chinese court emailing a party abroad often does not look like an Ontario court letter. There may be no firm letterhead and no full signature. Looking like a scam does not mean there is no file. The first practical step is to call the court’s filing office or clerk, during their business hours, with the court name and file number on the email or the envelope, and ask whether the case exists. The court is not obliged to give you legal advice in that thread. It wants formal process.

This is a Chinese civil proceeding. What has to be filed is a **defence**, not a reply to that email. If you oppose the divorce, the defence says the relationship has not broken down and you do not consent, with the evidence that court requires. A phone call or an email is usually not enough.

A lawyer sitting in Ontario **cannot** walk that defence into the Chinese court. The receiving court wants a litigation agent who can file locally, speak to the clerk, and appear on the date that court sets. Dual qualification helps on whether that court may have jurisdiction, whether service is complete, and what happens to Canadian property if the divorce goes through. It does not replace the filing. A power of attorney signed here generally has to be notarized in Ontario and then given a [Hague apostille](/en/hague-apostille/). China joined the Apostille Convention in 2023. An apostille is usually enough; consular legalization is no longer the default. The text has to match that court’s or that lawyer’s list. A wrong grant of authority means doing the package again.

Divorce is a status proceeding. China’s Civil Procedure Law still expects the party to appear even if they have counsel, unless they cannot express themselves; if they truly cannot attend, written submissions must go to the court. From overseas, a remote hearing or authenticated written submissions are the usual asks. The court sets the time. Counsel appearing does not automatically mean you never have to be seen.

## If I leave the parcel and ignore the email, does that stall the case?

Do not bet Ontario service rules.

Family-law service here is usually personal, with an acknowledgement. China is not. Mail, electronic service, leaving documents, and notice by publication each have their own rules. If you already replied to a court email, that reply can be used to show you knew there was a case. Collecting or not collecting the courier stack often does not change the sentence “were they served.”

Staying silent does not freeze the file. The larger risk is losing the chance to speak in that Chinese proceeding. If the claim asks only to dissolve the marriage, the court will not usually divide property that was never claimed. Once the marriage is dissolved, Ontario status and limitation change. That is the next section.

Don't treat delay as a plan. After a judgment refusing a divorce, the plaintiff who sues again within six months without new facts or new grounds can be refused. That is a limit on the plaintiff’s refiling. It is not a tool for you to hold the marriage open indefinitely. Where the claim relies on article 1079 of the Civil Code — including two years of separation due to incompatibility — **the first judgment can be a divorce**. Opposition belongs in a defence, with evidence. It does not belong in the assumption that China “never grants a divorce the first time.”

## Can the Chinese court divide the house in Canada?

It cannot transfer the land.

Ontario land sits on the Ontario register. Even if a Chinese judgment recites how foreign property should be split, the land titles office here will not change ownership on that paper. The other party does not take a Canadian house by obtaining a Chinese divorce. To divide property here, they have to instruct Ontario counsel and start an Ontario claim. They do not need a permanent-resident card. They do not need to board a plane.

The other way around: a Chinese pension, a Chinese deposit, a home still titled in China, is what that court can actually process. Counsel there can ask the court for an investigation order, and in some files for a freeze on accounts in China. If nobody claims property in that proceeding, the case is usually a divorce only. You can also choose not to counterclaim for a division in China. That is a different table from whether Ontario equalization is still open later.

Ontario equalization locks to the [separation date](/en/blog/separation-date-vs-divorce-date-ontario/), not the date on the Chinese judgment. A separation start the other party wrote into the Chinese pleading is an admission you may use later in Ontario. Keep the original. A long separation may already have run the six-year limb of s. 7(3). Once a Chinese divorce is recognized, the two-year limb from divorce is also in play. Do not concede a separation date in an email or a defence before that date has been mapped.

What Ontario can still do after the Chinese divorce, and how the clocks run, is in [Divorced in China — can Ontario still divide the property?](/en/blog/china-divorce-ontario-equalization-afterward/) and [limitation periods for equalization](/en/blog/equalization-limitation-chinese-lawyer-ontario/). Authority: [Cheng v. Liu, 2017 ONCA 104](https://www.canlii.org/en/on/onca/doc/2017/2017onca104/2017onca104.html). Recognition of the judgment is in [how China–Canada divorce judgments are recognized](/en/blog/china-canada-divorce-judgment-recognition/).

Citizenship or immigration status does not, by itself, take the Chinese divorce file away. If one party is domiciled or habitually resident in China, a foreign-related divorce may still be heard there. Whether the procedure and the service are complete is what to check first.

## Common questions

### Sued for divorce in China while you live in Canada. What now?

Treat it as a Chinese proceeding. File a defence, usually through Chinese counsel. An Ontario lawyer cannot file it there.

### Can dual qualification replace Chinese counsel?

Not for filing or appearing in that court. It can map jurisdiction and the Canadian property consequences, and it can prepare the apostilled power of attorney.

### I did not pick up the courier. Was I served?

Do not read it as Ontario service. Leaving the parcel does not mean there was no service. An email you answered can show you knew.

### If I oppose, is the first filing automatically dismissed?

No. A long-separation claim can produce a divorce the first time.

### Can the Chinese court divide the Canadian house?

It cannot transfer Ontario land. An Ontario claim is what divides property here.

### After the Chinese divorce, can they still equalize here?

There is a limitation. Section 7(3). Keep any separation date admitted in the Chinese pleading. See [Cheng v. Liu](https://www.canlii.org/en/on/onca/doc/2017/2017onca104/2017onca104.html).

### Does Canadian citizenship end the Chinese case?

No. Citizenship does not automatically remove that court’s process on a foreign-related divorce.
