No. Without a divorce, that half isn't legally yours yet — and no [will](/en/estate-trustee/with-a-will/) can lock in what you don't own. Finish the divorce first, get your share transferred into your own name, and then make a will. Only then can your children actually receive your portion.

## 1. Without a Divorce, "Your Half" Isn't Yours Yet

Everything is in your husband's name. The "half" you're thinking of is the [equalization](/en/blog/net-family-property-ontario/) amount a divorce would calculate — a claim, not [titled property](/en/blog/matrimonial-home-title-vs-property-division-ontario/) in your name.

A will can only dispose of what you own. If it's not in your name, writing it into your will changes nothing — you can't put someone else's property into your own will.

That equalization amount is still at the "doing the math" stage. Your husband holds the accounts and the money; your children are one layer removed. Whether they can collect, and how much, is entirely uncertain.

Who dies first changes everything. If your husband dies first, you as the spouse are the first-priority heir, with first claim on the property in his name. If you die first, a will leaving "your half" to the children does nothing — because the property is still in his name.

This is also where Canada differs from China. In China, the couple's property is first split fifty-fifty, and the deceased's half then goes through [succession](/en/estate-trustee/). Canada has no such pre-split procedure.

On top of that, the law gives the surviving spouse a choice: the spousal preferential share and the divorce-time equalization can be compared — whichever yields more is the one to take.

---

## 2. Without a Divorce, Your Husband as Spouse Comes First

[Without a divorce](/en/blog/separation-vs-divorce-ontario/), your husband is still your legal spouse. Under succession law, the spouse is the first-priority heir — children come after.

Adult children get even less special protection. Trying to use a will to pass "your half" straight to the children, over your husband's head, won't work — your husband as spouse can step in and claim it, and the children get nothing.

So without a divorce, the will route is a dead end.

---

## 3. The Right Order: Divorce First, Will After

Complete the divorce first, so the equalization actually happens and your share lands in your own name.

Once the money is in your name, make a will leaving it to your children — then it counts.

The order can't be reversed. Reversed, the will is just paper.

---

## What to Do

If this is your situation — in dispute, not ready to divorce, but knowing your husband won't provide for the children: a will can't help you, but two things can. First, for what you already own — name your children as [beneficiaries on your life insurance, RRSP, and TFSA](/en/blog/separation-agreement-waiver-still-need-will-ontario/). That money bypasses the estate entirely; it never passes through your husband's hands. Second, for your share of the property — you don't need a divorce to settle it. A [separation agreement](/en/blog/same-lawyer-both-sides-separation-agreement-ontario/) can fix your share and transfer it into your name. If he won't negotiate, litigation is the only route.

Don't count on "no divorce, just a will" to provide for your children. If the assets aren't in your name, a will can't arrange them.

If you truly want your share to go to your children, get the divorce done first and take what's yours.

Once your share is in hand, make a separate will spelling out what goes to the children.

Dragging out the marriage without divorcing leaves every variable in your husband's hands.

## FAQs

### 1. Is it valid to write "half of my husband's property goes to the children" in my will?

No. You can only dispose of property in your own name. What's in your husband's name doesn't count, no matter what your will says.

### 2. Without a divorce, how much can my husband take as spouse?

The spouse is the first-priority heir, ahead of the children. Adult children have even less special protection. The share you wanted for your children, your husband can claim and take.

### 3. Should I divorce first or make a will first?

Divorce first. Get your share into your own name, then make a will. Reversed, the will can't lock anything in.
