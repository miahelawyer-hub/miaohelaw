No, you do not have to repay it. The company borrowed the money, not you, and you signed no personal guarantee, so the creditor cannot come after you. But the loan still reduces what you receive. It is deducted from your spouse's [net family property](/en/blog/net-family-property-ontario/) on the date of separation, which shrinks the [equalization](/en/family-law/property-division/) payment.

A client told me the story. Her husband runs a company. The company borrowed money from the bank. She never signed a personal guarantee. Now they are separating, and she is concerned about whether she needs to repay the company's loan.

She does not have to repay it, but she may receive less.

---

## 1. The company is a separate legal person

A corporation is a separate legal person. It owns its assets and owes its debts. When the company borrows money, the company owes the money, not you and not your spouse personally. The creditor can go after the company's assets. It cannot go after your personal assets. The only exception is a personal guarantee. If you signed one, you promised to pay if the company does not. Then the creditor can come after you. You did not sign one. So the debt is not yours.

---

## 2. First look at who owns the company

If your spouse owns all of it, the whole value of the company goes into your spouse's net family property. If there are other shareholders, only the value of your spouse's shares goes in. The company's value is what counts, not the company's debts alone. A company with a big loan can still be worth something, if its assets are worth more than what it owes. Or it can be worth nothing, if the loan is bigger than the assets. Either way, what goes into your spouse's net family property is [the value of the shares](/en/blog/company-in-my-name-never-looked-books-divorce-ontario/), not the loan itself.

---

## 3. Here is how equalization works in Ontario

On the date of separation, each spouse adds up their net family property, what they own minus what they owe. The spouse with the higher number pays the other half the difference. That is the equalization payment. Now suppose your spouse's net family property is reduced to zero by the company's loan, or close to it, while your own net family property is still positive. You are the spouse with the higher number, so you pay your spouse half the difference. You never owed the company's debt. But the loan has already done its work inside your spouse's net family property, and you end up with less.

---

## 4. This is how the loan shrinks your spouse's net family property

Take a simple example. Say the company owns $500,000 in assets and owes $300,000 on a loan. A company is worth its assets minus its liabilities, so this company is worth $200,000. That $200,000 goes into your spouse's net family property. Without the loan, the company would be worth the full $500,000, and your spouse's net family property would be $300,000 higher. The debt does not disappear. It comes straight off the value of the company, and that is how it shrinks your spouse's net family property.

---

## 5. Not having to repay the loan and receiving less are two different things

The creditor question turns on the paperwork. The loan is in the company's name, and you signed no personal guarantee, so the creditor cannot come after you. The equalization question is separate. It comes down to the numbers on the date of separation. The loan is deducted on your spouse's side, which makes your spouse's net family property smaller, and that leaves less for you.

---

## Frequently asked questions

### 1. If I did sign a personal guarantee, what happens?

Then the creditor can come after you. A personal guarantee is a promise that you will pay if the company does not. The creditor does not have to wait. It can collect from you directly.

### 2. Who decides what the company is worth?

A business valuator does. The company is valued as of the date of separation. If the two sides disagree, each side hires its own valuator, and the judge accepts the valuation that is more reasonable. If the two sides can agree on a number, the court will use that number.

### 3. What if the company was started before the marriage?

Only the increase counts. The company's value on the date of marriage is deducted. What goes into net family property is the growth in value between the date of marriage and the date of separation.
