You do not need to fly back to China. Sign a [power of attorney](/en/hague-apostille/china-property-power-of-attorney/) in Canada, have it notarized and [apostilled](/en/hague-apostille/) here, and send the original to China. Your agent can then sign the sale contract and handle the transfer. The key is the wording. The registry in China only accepts a power of attorney in its own format.

A client came in recently. She owns an apartment in China and has found a buyer, but she cannot go back. Work, children, and the cost of the trip make it impractical. She asked whether someone in China could sell it for her, and what she needed to sign here in Canada.

A power of attorney signed in Canada, notarized and apostilled, lets someone in China sell the house for you.

---

## 1. What the power of attorney must say

The registry in China checks the document against its own requirements. The power of attorney must name the principal and the agent, with their identity document numbers. It must identify the property precisely: the address exactly as it appears on the title certificate, and the certificate number. It must list the authorized acts one by one: signing the sale contract, handling the transfer, paying tax, collecting the sale proceeds. It must state the term of the appointment. And it must say clearly whether the agent may sign the sale contract. Vague authority gets the document rejected. A general statement such as "handle all matters relating to the house" is not enough.

---

## 2. The format follows the receiving office

Different cities in China have different requirements for a power of attorney. Some registries have their own template. Some want specific wording for the authorized acts. Some want the authority to collect the sale proceeds spelled out. Before the document is drafted, the receiving office's requirements should be confirmed. A power of attorney drafted to the wrong format will be sent back, and the whole process starts again. Our office drafts to the receiving office's format, and recent powers of attorney for selling, buying and registering title in China were accepted on the first pass.

---

## 3. The process in Canada

The power of attorney is drafted in Chinese and English. You come to the Markham office once to sign it. It is [notarized](/en/notary-public/) here, then apostilled by Ontario Official Documents Services. Since both Canada and China joined the Hague Apostille Convention, there is no more consular legalization at the Chinese embassy or consulate. The apostilled original is then sent to China, where your agent takes it to the registry. Urgent files can be ready the next day.

---

## 4. Things to watch

Choose your agent carefully. A power of attorney lets them sign contracts and collect money in your name. If the house is jointly owned, every owner must agree to the sale. An owner who cannot go back signs a power of attorney. If you have changed your name or your passport since the house was bought, the registry may also want a [same-name declaration](/en/hague-apostille/same-name-declaration/) proving you are the same person. And a power of attorney can be revoked. If you change your mind, revoke it in writing before the agent acts.

---

## Frequently asked questions

### 1. Must the agent be a relative?

No. The agent can be a family member, a friend, or a lawyer in China. The registry does not require a family relationship. What matters is that the agent is someone you trust, because the power of attorney lets them sign and collect money in your name.

### 2. Does a power of attorney expire?

Yes, you set the term in the document. It can run until the sale is complete, or to a fixed date. It can also be revoked at any time before the agent acts. If the term runs out before the sale closes, a new power of attorney is needed.

### 3. The house is in both spouses' names. Can one spouse sign the power of attorney alone?

No. A sale of jointly owned property needs both owners' agreement. The spouse who cannot go back signs a power of attorney. The spouse in China signs at the registry. If only one owner signs, the registry will not process the transfer.
