From Canada, the documents for inheriting or renouncing property in China need a [Hague Apostille](/en/hague-apostille/) before they can be used in China. Renouncing takes a [renunciation declaration](/en/hague-apostille/renunciation-of-inheritance/). Inheriting takes [proof of the parent-child relationship](/en/hague-apostille/parent-child-declaration/). That proof has no expiry date, so it can be prepared in advance.

We help many clients with Hague Apostilles. A large part of that work is inheritance: someone in Canada renouncing an inheritance in China, or someone in Canada trying to inherit property there. The paperwork looks simple, but it is the step where inheritances get stuck.

The documents can be prepared in advance. Do not wait until a relative has passed away.

---

## 1. Renouncing an Inheritance from Canada

If you live in Canada and want to renounce an inheritance in China, you must make a declaration of renunciation. After the Hague Apostille, the person in China who is inheriting can take that document to the notary office and handle the inheritance directly. Without it, the inheritance cannot move forward, because the notary needs every heir's position in writing.

---

## 2. Inheriting Property in China Takes Proof of the Parent-Child Relationship

If you live in Canada and want to inherit real estate in China, China requires proof that you are the deceased's child. In our apostille work for clients, the documents that can prove this include the household registration book, showing you were once on the same household registration in China, the birth certificate, and the parent-child relationship certificate many people made when they immigrated. All of these can serve as proof.

Have those documents notarized in advance. Do not wait. Some clients have faced this difficulty: if the government office loses its digital data or loses all the hard copies, it cannot even provide you with the proof, and you will fall into trouble trying to figure it out. We have already received complaints from clients about the Chinese government losing their data, leaving them stuck and unable to proceed.

---

## 3. The Proof Has No Expiry Date. Prepare It in Advance

A parent-child relationship certificate has no expiry date, so this is something you can do early. It matters most for people who were born in China and came to Canada, who once held Chinese identity documents and later switched to a Canadian passport or [changed their name](/en/blog/same-person-declaration-after-death-ontario/). In those cases, the parent-child proof becomes especially important, because the names and documents no longer line up on their own. We have seen two cases where the heirs could not prove they were the deceased's relatives, and the inheritance has still not been completed after three or four years.

Do not wait until a relative has passed away. While they are still alive, they can even write a parent-child relationship declaration themselves. Once they are gone, no one can write that declaration for them. The police no longer issue kinship certificates, and old household registration books get lost or moved to the archives. What is easy to prepare now can take months of digging later.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can I still get the proof if my parents have already passed away?

Yes, but it is harder. The notary pieces the relationship together from the old household registration book, the birth certificate, and personnel files. The police no longer issue kinship certificates, so if the old papers are gone, the last resort is to ask the court to confirm the relationship. That is why we tell clients to prepare early.

### 2. I am already a Canadian citizen. Can I still inherit a house in China?

Yes. Foreign nationality does not affect your right to inherit property in China. The procedure takes an extra step: documents issued in Canada need an Apostille before they can be used in China.

### 3. How long does a parent-child relationship certificate last?

It has no expiry date. Once it is done, it stays valid. That is why it makes sense to prepare it early, while your parents are alive and the papers are easy to find.
