Yes. The money in the RESP is yours — you earned it and deposited it. "It's for my child's university" doesn't put it beyond the bank's reach. If you don't pay your debts, the bank can freeze your accounts, RESP included. Only one situation stops them: the money has already been paid out for your child's education.

The [matrimonial home](/en/blog/matrimonial-home-title-vs-property-division-ontario/) is in the husband's name alone, and so is the mortgage. The market has turned, the house has lost value, and the monthly payments are no longer affordable. The wife wants to ring-fence the family's other assets — starting with the RESP set aside for their child's university.

---

## 1. "The Child's Money" Is Still Your Money

The RESP names your child as beneficiary, but you opened the account and you made the deposits. You earned that money, one contribution at a time.

"It's for my child's university" is your intention, not a legal lock. The bank looks at one thing: whose money is it.

If you don't pay what you owe, the bank can freeze your accounts — the RESP included. No law says "education money for the kids" is off limits.

---

## 2. Only One Situation Stops the Bank

The money has already been paid out for education — your child used it for tuition and living expenses. It's no longer in your hands, so there's nothing for the bank to go after.

Money still sitting in the account is still your property, even if your child starts university tomorrow. The bank can still reach it.

So "it's not time yet" is not a shield. Unused means it's still yours — and still reachable.

---

## 3. Removing a Name or Signing a Separation Agreement Won't Stop the Bank

Taking your husband's name off the RESP and leaving only yours: that doesn't ring-fence anything. The matrimonial home was bought during the marriage, the family has lived in it throughout the marriage, and the mortgage debt was incurred during the marriage too. Even with the name removed and a [separation agreement](/en/blog/same-lawyer-both-sides-separation-agreement-ontario/) signed, this debt is in substance the debt of both spouses — the bank has legal ground to pursue you.

Writing "the RESP is mine" in a separation agreement: that's between the two of you. The bank is an outsider — your agreement doesn't bind it. Especially where the debt arose before separation.

The house debt works the same way: title may be in your husband's name alone, but it's the matrimonial home and you live there and benefit from it, so the bank can pursue you too.

---

## What to Do

Don't count on the RESP to hide money. If things reach the point of enforcement, it can't be hidden.

If you want to protect assets, act before the debt spirals. Once the bank moves, no ring-fencing will help.

The most reliable way to protect it is to keep the debt from getting that far: talk to the bank early if you're falling behind — don't wait until accounts are frozen.

Once your child reaches university age and the money is withdrawn for genuine education expenses, it's outside the bank's reach.
