No. Signing the agreement does not end your rights — it depends on how the agreement is drafted. Drafted well, your rights survive until the buyout is paid in full.

A client consulted me: the [matrimonial home](/en/blog/house-in-his-name-designate-matrimonial-home-ontario/) is in his name, and his ex is asking him to sign a [separation agreement](/en/blog/only-i-disclosed-separation-agreement-ontario/) that requires him to [transfer title](/en/blog/house-transferred-cheap-after-separation-ontario/) as soon as the agreement is signed. As drafted, the agreement releases his rights the moment he signs, before he has received the full buyout payment — and that is what he is afraid of.

The short answer: whether your rights survive signing depends on how the agreement is drafted. Drafted well, there is no problem — and this is exactly where a lawyer's experience shows.

---

## 1. The Release of Rights Should Take Effect When the Buyout Is Paid in Full — Not When You Sign

You can draft the separation agreement the way you want it — including when your release of rights takes effect. In this case, the client can include a specific clause stating that he releases his rights only once he has received the full buyout payment.

It is also common to set out the payment terms in the agreement: how the buyout is paid, in how many installments, and within how many years the payment must be completed.

When a separation agreement addresses all of these issues, it sets clear expectations for both sides and is less likely to be found unenforceable. Once the agreement is certain and enforceable, the court can enforce it even if a party raises a dispute later — and that protects you, saves legal costs, gives you certainty, and gives the judge a clear basis on which to decide.

---

## 2. Register a Charge on the House After the Transfer

A charge is a debt registered on title: the house is transferred to her, but she still owes you money. When the money is paid in full, you hire a lawyer and discharge the charge at your own cost. With a charge registered, the house cannot disappear. If she sells, any lawyer acting on the sale will see the charge and must contact you first, obtain a payout statement, and pay what she owes you out of the sale proceeds before closing.

If she wants to refinance the property, she still needs your consent, because the charge is on title. A refinance must either postpone your charge behind the new mortgage or discharge it and register a new one — and neither can happen without you. That means you are in control of the situation: she cannot sell the property without your consent or knowledge. You do not need to worry that the property will disappear and leave you unable to recover your payment.

---

## 3. Fix a Payment Deadline Each Year — and Give Yourself a Remedy If It Is Missed

State the date by which each year's payment must be made. If the date passes without payment, send a written notice giving her a deadline, for example fifteen days. If she still does not pay, you have the option of [applying to sell the house](/en/blog/force-sale-matrimonial-home-ontario/) and recovering everything still owed from the proceeds in one payment. Do not wait until the final year to act — the earlier you move, the more control you keep. In this way, you also set a firm deadline for the other party: if she fails to make the payment, she will know in advance what the consequences are and how they affect her rights.

---

## 4. Do Not Accept a Clause That Waits Until the Final Year to Sell

One reminder: do not agree to a clause that lets you act only in the final year. You may need the money sooner — for example, you may need it right away — but if you have agreed that a sale can only be requested in the final year, then when you need the money, there is nothing you can do. If you go to court, you will still have to argue the point and spend a great deal on legal costs, because the agreement provides no certain relief — and the court has nothing to enforce. Draft the agreement properly, and you keep the right to demand the money whenever you need it.

## FAQs

### 1. What if she sells the house early — what happens to my buyout?

With a charge registered, the sale proceeds pay you first. The agreement can also state that an early sale triggers immediate payment of the remaining buyout in full.

### 2. She says "once you sign, the house is mine." Is that right?

No. Transferring title only changes the name on the deed; your interest in the house remains. If the agreement is properly drafted, your rights survive until the buyout is paid in full.

### 3. What if the agreement was signed without financial disclosure?

A [separation agreement](/en/blog/only-i-disclosed-separation-agreement-ontario/) signed without financial disclosure may not be upheld by the court. Whether to do full disclosure is a question of time and cost, but the risk should be understood before signing.
