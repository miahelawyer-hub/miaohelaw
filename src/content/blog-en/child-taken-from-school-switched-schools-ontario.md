Yes. The court will generally order the child returned to their original life and their original school. Taking the child first doesn't mean winning — the court looks at who the child was living with and where they were going to school, restores the routine that was interrupted, and only then deals with future arrangements.

A client came to consult me. In her case, the child had been living with her and attending the original school. One day, her spouse picked the child up from school and didn't bring the child back — the child was moved to a different school, and she wasn't allowed to see them. She brought an [urgent motion](/en/blog/urgent-motion-equalization-mid-case-ontario/), and the judge ordered the child returned to her and to the original school.

The short answer: yes, the child comes back. Taking first is not winning.

---

## 1. The Status Quo Is Who the Child Lived With and Where They Went to School

When the court deals with something like this, it starts with the child's life as it was: who the child had been living with, which school they attended, who was looking after the day-to-day care. That is the [status quo](/en/blog/sole-custody-vs-child-lives-with-me-ontario/). When one parent picks the child up from school, switches schools, and cuts off contact, what changed was changed by that parent's own hand — not by any court. A change you make yourself is not one the court treats as the new normal. What he interrupted is exactly what the court protects: stability in the child's life.

---

## 2. An Urgent Motion Restores the Status Quo — It Doesn't Award the Child to Anyone

The court moves quickly on a motion like this, because a child's situation can't wait: where a child goes to school today and where they sleep tonight can't sit in a queue for months. The judge's direction on the motion was clear: the child returns to the mother and to the original school. Notice what the judge did not do — the judge did not decide the child "belongs" to either parent. An urgent motion does one thing: it puts the interrupted routine back the way it was. The final arrangements are worked out later, through the rest of the process.

---

## 3. The Return Is Only a Temporary Arrangement — Not the Final Judgment

What an urgent motion does is temporarily protect the child's rights and preserve the child's status quo. It is not the final decision. How much [parenting time](/en/blog/child-custody-ontario-chinese/) the father has, and how it is arranged, is determined later in the process. Nor will the court generally refuse the father time with the child because of behaviour like this — the court will confirm that he, too, has parenting time with the child. The governing principle in our law is the child's best interests, and those interests include spending meaningful time with each parent. So the motion settles only one thing — the child comes back for now; how time with the child is shared is decided afterwards.

So if this happens to you — your spouse takes your child — don't panic, and don't use the same tactic to take the child back. Bring an urgent motion right away. The longer you wait, the less urgent it looks to the court, and the weaker your position becomes. Put the child's original life in front of the judge — who the child lived with, where they went to school. When those facts are clear, the court will generally order the child returned first.

---

## FAQs

### 1. My spouse took my child. Can I go to his home or the new school and take the child back myself?

Don't. Taking the child back yourself invites confrontation, frightens the child, and can make the situation worse. Go through the court: bring an urgent motion and let a judge order the return. A court order is something your spouse must comply with.

### 2. The child has already been at the new school for a while. Can the child still be transferred back?

It depends on what the child's life looked like before, and how much time has passed. When it has just happened, the court will generally restore the original arrangement. The longer it drags on, the more your spouse will argue the child has settled into the new school. Speed matters — the earlier the motion, the better.

### 3. Once the child is returned, does that mean my spouse can't see the child anymore?

No. The return is a temporary arrangement that preserves the child's original routine. The father's parenting time is determined later in the process, and the court won't deny him time with the child because he took the child — the child's best interests include spending time with each parent.
