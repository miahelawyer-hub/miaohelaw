You cannot deduct the [matrimonial home](/en/blog/premarital-home-wedding-day-value-divorce-ontario/)'s value — its full value goes into the calculation. But [premarital property](/en/blog/premarital-home-sold-ontario-deduction/) can be deducted at its date-of-marriage value: the deduction covers what you owned when you married.

A client came to consult me. Her husband owned a condo before the marriage. He sold it after they married and used the proceeds to buy their matrimonial home. The matrimonial home was purchased after the marriage, in both their names. Now they are divorcing, and her question is: can the money from his condo be treated as his premarital property and deducted from the matrimonial home?

The short answer: the matrimonial home's value cannot be deducted, but premarital property can be deducted at its date-of-marriage value.

---

## 1. The Deduction Turns on What You Owned on the Date of Marriage

Ontario divides the growth in each spouse's net worth during the marriage: what you owned on the date of separation, less what you owned on the date of marriage. The difference is what gets equalized. The rule is in section 4(1) of the Family Law Act: [net family property](/en/blog/net-family-property-ontario/) is the value of property owned on the valuation date, less debts, less the value of property — other than a matrimonial home — owned on the date of the marriage.

The deduction is for net value, not the purchase price: the market value on the date of marriage, less the mortgage and any other debts owing on that date. And the spouse claiming the deduction bears the onus of proving it (s. 4(3)) — the date-of-marriage appraisal, the mortgage statement, the sale records. Without that evidence, there is no deduction.

The matrimonial home is excepted in the words of the statute itself — "other than a matrimonial home" — and whether a property is a matrimonial home is determined at the valuation date: the family residence you were still living in at separation. The Court of Appeal confirmed the point in Knight v. Knight-Kerr, 2021 ONCA 686: a home brought into the marriage that remains the matrimonial home at separation gets no date-of-marriage deduction, but if it was sold during the marriage and the family moved elsewhere, so that it was no longer the matrimonial home at separation, its date-of-marriage value is deductible.

So there is a single precondition: the property must still have been yours on the date of marriage.

---

## 2. The Timeline: the Condo Predates the Marriage and Was Sold After It

He owned the condo before the marriage, and it was still in his name when they married. After the marriage, he sold it and used the proceeds to buy another house. Because the condo was his on the date of marriage, its value is deductible — claim the date-of-marriage deduction, for its net value on that date. What cannot be deducted is the matrimonial home's value: the house bought with the proceeds is now the matrimonial home, and its full value goes into the calculation.

---

## 3. Buy the Matrimonial Home First and Marry Afterwards, and the Deduction Is Lost

If the matrimonial home was already purchased and the condo already sold by the date of marriage — in other words, they bought the home first and married afterwards — the condo's premarital value cannot be deducted.

The reason is straightforward: the date-of-marriage deduction is a snapshot of what you owned on the date of marriage. Whatever you owned then is deductible; whatever you did not own then is not. By the date of marriage, the condo was already sold. The snapshot shows no condo — only the matrimonial home.

The condo proceeds went into the matrimonial home.

By the date of marriage, the condo no longer existed, so there was no value left to deduct.

## FAQs

### 1. The matrimonial home was bought before the marriage. Can its date-of-marriage value be deducted?

No. Whenever the matrimonial home was bought, its date-of-marriage value cannot be deducted — the full value goes into the calculation.

### 2. The condo was still owned on the date of marriage and sold after the marriage. How much is deducted?

Its net value on the date of marriage — the market value at the time, less the mortgage still owing then — not the price it later sold for. You will need evidence from the date of marriage: an appraisal, the sale records, the mortgage statement.

### 3. The condo was bought before the marriage and lived in right up to separation. Can its date-of-marriage value be deducted?

No. If it was still the family residence on the date of separation, the whole property counts as the matrimonial home, and none of its date-of-marriage value can be deducted. Only if it was sold, vacated, or rented out before separation might the deduction become available again.
